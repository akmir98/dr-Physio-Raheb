Understanding Knee's Pain

Knee pain can come from weak muscles, joint strain, or a previous injury that never fully healed.

We assess knee alignment and surrounding muscle strength to relieve pain and rebuild stability.

We assess strength, alignment, and movement throughout the whole leg, since knee pain is very often influenced by the hips and ankles as much as the knee joint itself.

  • Pain while walking or climbing stairs
  • Stiffness after sitting
  • Swelling around the knee
  • Reduced knee stability

What Could Be Causing Your Knee's Pain?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Weak Supporting Muscles

Weakness in the quadriceps and hamstrings increases stress on the knee joint. Strengthening these muscles is one of the most effective ways to reduce ongoing knee strain.

Joint Strain

Repetitive impact or overloading can strain the structures within the knee. Gradually increasing activity levels, rather than sudden spikes, helps protect the joint.

Previous Injury

An old knee injury that wasn't fully rehabilitated can lead to recurring pain. Without proper rehabilitation, old injuries often leave the knee more vulnerable to repeat strain.

Poor Movement Patterns

Uneven gait or movement mechanics places excess stress on one knee. Correcting gait early can prevent uneven wear from becoming a long-term issue.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is knee pain always related to arthritis?

No — many cases of knee pain, especially in younger or active patients, are related to muscle weakness or movement patterns rather than joint degeneration.

Should I avoid stairs and squats?

Not necessarily. We'll assess which movements to modify temporarily and which ones actually support your recovery.

How soon can I return to sport?

This depends on the cause and severity of your knee pain, but many patients follow a gradual, guided return-to-sport plan over several weeks.
