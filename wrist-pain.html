Understanding Wrist Pain

Daily keyboard and phone use is a growing cause of wrist pain and dysfunction, including carpal tunnel-related discomfort.

We assess wrist and forearm mechanics to relieve pressure and restore comfortable, pain-free movement.

We assess your wrist, forearm, and even your posture at your workstation, since wrist pain is frequently connected to habits and positioning that go far beyond the wrist itself.

  • Pain while typing or gripping
  • Numbness or tingling in the hand
  • Stiffness in the morning
  • Reduced wrist strength

What Could Be Causing Your Wrist Pain?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Repetitive Strain

Prolonged typing or phone use places repetitive stress on the wrist tendons. Small adjustments to hand position during typing can significantly reduce cumulative strain.

Poor Ergonomics

Incorrect wrist positioning during work increases pressure on the nerves and tendons. Improving your setup at work or home is often one of the most effective long-term fixes.

Overuse

Repetitive gripping or lifting can gradually strain the wrist joint. This is especially common in jobs requiring repeated gripping, twisting, or lifting motions.

Nerve Compression

Swelling or tightness around the wrist can compress nerves, causing pain or numbness. Addressing this early can help prevent numbness or tingling from becoming persistent.

Frequently Asked Questions

Could this be carpal tunnel syndrome?

Numbness or tingling alongside wrist pain can point toward nerve involvement, including carpal tunnel syndrome. A proper assessment can confirm this and guide treatment.

Do I need to stop typing completely?

Usually not — we'll help you adjust technique, posture, and breaks rather than stop entirely, unless your case requires more rest.

Will a wrist brace help?

A brace can provide short-term support, especially at night, but it works best combined with targeted exercises rather than as a standalone fix.
