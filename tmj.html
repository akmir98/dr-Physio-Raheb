TMJ Syndrome

TMJ dysfunction can make eating, talking, and even resting your jaw uncomfortable. Physiotherapy helps restore normal jaw movement without drugs or surgery. Because the jaw joint works closely with the neck and facial muscles, TMJ issues rarely stay isolated — which is why we look at the whole picture, not just the joint itself.

Understanding TMJ Syndrome

The temporomandibular joint (TMJ) connects your jaw to your skull, and dysfunction here can cause pain that spreads to your head, neck, and shoulders.

We assess jaw alignment and surrounding muscle tension to relieve pressure and restore comfortable movement.

Our assessment includes checking how your jaw opens and closes, how evenly it moves, and how much tension has built up in the surrounding muscles, so treatment addresses the actual mechanics of the problem.

  • Clicking or popping in the jaw
  • Pain while chewing or talking
  • Jaw stiffness in the morning
  • Headaches linked to jaw tension

What Could Be Causing Your TMJ Syndrome?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Jaw Clenching

Habitual clenching or grinding places ongoing strain on the TMJ and surrounding muscles. Many patients aren't even aware they clench or grind until the strain shows up as pain.

Muscle Tension

Tight jaw and facial muscles restrict smooth joint movement and increase discomfort. This tension often builds gradually, making the cause easy to overlook until symptoms appear.

Joint Misalignment

An uneven bite or joint alignment can cause the TMJ to move unevenly under load. Even small misalignments can cause the joint to work harder than it should on one side.

Stress

Stress often shows up as unconscious jaw tension, worsening TMJ symptoms over time. Because stress-related clenching often happens during sleep, it can be difficult to notice on your own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need surgery for TMJ syndrome?

Most cases of TMJ dysfunction respond well to conservative physiotherapy and don't require surgery. We focus on restoring healthy joint movement and reducing muscle tension first.

Will treatment stop the clicking sound in my jaw?

Clicking can improve as joint mechanics and muscle balance improve, though in some cases the sound may remain even after pain has resolved. We'll discuss realistic expectations during your assessment.

Can stress make TMJ pain worse?

Yes — stress often leads to unconscious jaw clenching, which can significantly worsen TMJ symptoms. We include relaxation techniques as part of a complete treatment plan.
