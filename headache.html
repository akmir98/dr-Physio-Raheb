Understanding Headaches

Headaches are often linked to poor posture, muscle tension in the neck and shoulders, or stress-related tightness rather than the head itself.

Our physiotherapists assess your posture and neck mobility to identify what's triggering your headaches, then build a plan around it.

Because headaches can have several contributing factors at once, we don't rely on guesswork. A thorough movement and posture assessment helps us understand your specific pattern, so the treatment plan actually matches the cause.

  • Frequent tension around the temples
  • Pain worsens with screen time
  • Tightness in neck and shoulders
  • Headaches linked to poor posture

What Could Be Causing Your Headaches?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Poor Posture

Prolonged screen time and desk work place strain on the neck, often referring pain to the head. Left unaddressed, this postural strain becomes a recurring trigger rather than an occasional one.

Muscle Tension

Tight muscles in the neck and scalp are one of the most common drivers of recurring headaches. Over time, this tension can become chronic if the underlying posture and movement habits aren't corrected.

Poor Sleep Posture

Sleeping positions that strain the neck can trigger headaches that appear each morning. Because it happens overnight, this cause is often the hardest for patients to recognize on their own.

Sedentary Lifestyle

Lack of movement reduces circulation and increases muscular tightness that contributes to headaches. Combined with tight muscles, inactivity can turn an occasional headache into a frequent one.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can physiotherapy really help with headaches?

Yes — many headaches, especially tension-type headaches, are linked to the neck, posture, and muscle tension rather than the head itself. Addressing these areas often reduces both the frequency and intensity of headaches.

How many sessions will I need?

This depends on how long you've had the headaches and what's causing them. Many patients notice a difference within the first few sessions, with a full plan typically spanning several weeks.

Is treatment painful?

Most techniques are gentle and well-tolerated. Some manual therapy around tight areas may feel slightly uncomfortable at first, but it shouldn't be sharply painful.
