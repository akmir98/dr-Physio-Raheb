Understanding Mid-Back Pain

Mid-back pain often develops from prolonged sitting, poor posture, or tightness between the shoulder blades.

We assess your thoracic spine mobility and posture to relieve tension and build lasting strength.

We assess how your spine moves as a whole, since mid-back pain is often connected to stiffness or weakness elsewhere — commonly the shoulders, neck, or lower back — rather than an isolated problem.

  • Stiffness between the shoulder blades
  • Pain after prolonged sitting
  • Discomfort with deep breathing
  • Reduced upper back mobility

What Could Be Causing Your Mid-Back Pain?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Prolonged Sitting

Long periods of sitting, especially with poor posture, strain the mid-back muscles. The longer this posture is held daily, the more the surrounding muscles adapt to compensate — often painfully.

Desk Posture

A forward-hunched posture at a desk places sustained load on the thoracic spine. This posture gradually restricts natural spinal movement, making stiffness worse over time.

Muscle Imbalance

Weak upper-back muscles combined with tight chest muscles pull posture out of alignment. This imbalance is one of the most common — and most correctable — causes of mid-back discomfort.

Repetitive Strain

Repeated bending or twisting motions can gradually irritate the mid-back. These motions place uneven load on the thoracic spine, especially when done repeatedly without proper form.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why does my mid-back hurt when I breathe deeply?

This is often linked to stiffness in the joints connecting your ribs to your spine. Improving thoracic mobility usually eases this discomfort.

Is mid-back pain linked to my desk job?

Very often, yes. Prolonged sitting and forward-hunched posture are among the most common causes we see for this type of pain.

Will stretching alone fix this?

Stretching helps, but lasting relief usually requires strengthening the muscles that support upright posture too — not just loosening tight ones.
