Understanding Heel Pain

Heel pain is one of the most common foot complaints, often caused by inflammation, repetitive strain, or poor movement mechanics.

Our physiotherapists assess your gait, footwear, and muscle function to pinpoint the exact source of your pain — then build a plan around it.

Because heel pain often has more than one contributing factor, we take time to assess your gait, footwear, and calf flexibility together — so your treatment plan addresses the real cause, not just the symptom.

  • Pain while walking
  • Morning heel discomfort
  • Pain after prolonged standing
  • Reduced mobility

What Could Be Causing Your Heel Pain?

Understanding the root cause is the first step toward lasting relief.

Plantar Fasciitis

Inflammation of the tissue connecting your heel to your toes, often causing sharp pain with the first steps in the morning. This is why the sharp first-step pain in the morning is such a common and telling symptom.

Overuse

Repetitive impact from walking, running, or standing for long periods can gradually strain the heel and surrounding tissue. Supportive footwear and gradual load management can make a significant difference here.

Tight Muscles

Tight calf muscles and the Achilles tendon place extra tension on the heel, increasing pain and reducing flexibility. Loosening these structures often brings noticeable relief within the first few sessions.

Poor Movement Patterns

Improper gait or footwear can shift pressure unevenly across the foot, placing excess strain on the heel over time. Correcting this early can prevent the strain from shifting to other parts of the foot.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is this plantar fasciitis?

Sharp heel pain, especially with the first steps in the morning, is a classic sign of plantar fasciitis. A proper assessment can confirm this and rule out other causes.

How long does heel pain usually take to improve?

Many patients notice improvement within a few weeks of consistent treatment, though full recovery depends on how long the issue has been present.

Should I change my shoes?

Supportive, well-cushioned footwear often helps significantly. We can advise on what to look for based on your specific foot mechanics.
